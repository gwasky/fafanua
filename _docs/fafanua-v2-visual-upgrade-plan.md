# Fafanua Website — Version 2 Visual Upgrade Plan

## Objective

Upgrade the current Fafanua Technologies website from a clean but visually simple consultancy site into a more distinctive, premium data and analytics technology website.

This is a **visual and interaction upgrade**, not a content-strategy rewrite.

Preserve the approved service architecture, service copy, lifecycle labels, Managed Data & Analytics positioning, Solutions structure, How We Work, Reverse ETL positioning, and future-ready messaging.

The desired shift is:

> **From:** clean corporate consultancy website  
> **To:** premium data infrastructure and analytics technology company

Use external references only for design principles such as hierarchy, atmosphere, typography scale, technical-grid treatment, contrast, rhythm, and confidence. Do not copy another website's branding, layout, assets, or wording.

---

# 1. Preserve Existing Decisions

Do not rewrite the approved services content.

Retain:

- six core service cards;
- lifecycle labels: **Design, Connect, Model, Trust, Govern, Decide**;
- “Typical engagements” accordions;
- Managed Data & Analytics Services;
- Reverse ETL and operational data activation;
- five-sector Solutions section;
- How We Work;
- one future-ready section;
- future-ready heading: **Trusted foundations for what comes next**;
- no separate AI capability grid;
- no presentation of Fafanua Intelligence as an existing product.

This task is primarily about **presentation, visual hierarchy, page rhythm, and interaction**.

---

# 2. Core Design Principle

Use this rule throughout the redesign:

> **Do not add decoration to make the site feel premium. Increase hierarchy, scale, contrast, rhythm, and intentional whitespace.**

The site should feel engineered rather than embellished.

Avoid:

- decorative clutter;
- generic AI graphics;
- floating blobs;
- excessive gradients;
- heavy glassmorphism;
- particle effects;
- over-animation;
- repeated card grids for every section;
- fake dashboards or speculative product screenshots.

---

# 3. Brand System

Retain the existing Fafanua identity:

- Graphite;
- Paper / warm off-white;
- Signal teal;
- existing service-line colours.

Do not introduce another accent colour simply to imitate a reference site.

Signal teal should remain scarce and purposeful.

Use teal for:

- primary CTA emphasis;
- small highlights;
- key words where appropriate;
- focus states;
- subtle lines and markers;
- restrained interaction accents.

Do not make large sections teal.

---

# 4. Target Page Rhythm

Target sequence:

1. Header / navigation
2. Dark technical hero
3. Light positioning section
4. Capabilities / Services
5. Managed Data & Analytics
6. Solutions
7. How We Work
8. Dark future-ready section
9. About
10. Closing contact CTA
11. Footer

The page should not read as:

> heading → cards → heading → cards → heading → cards

Each major section should have a composition appropriate to its purpose.

---

# 5. Hero Redesign

## Goal

Make the hero the strongest visual moment on the site.

Target desktop height:

- approximately **80–90vh**;
- natural but substantial height on mobile.

## Background

Use a dark graphite surface with a subtle technical grid.

The grid should:

- be low contrast;
- feel architectural;
- sit behind the content;
- remain visually quiet;
- scale cleanly across viewports;
- preferably be implemented using lightweight CSS.

Do not use a raster background simply to create the grid.

## Headline

Preferred direction:

> **Trusted Data.**  
> **Better Decisions.**

Core brand promise, directly beneath the headline:

> Build a data foundation you can trust.

The hierarchy is: the headline (the `h1`, the primary visual proposition); then the core brand promise, a prominent paragraph, not a heading, so the heading outline is unchanged, smaller than the `h1` and larger than the supporting copy; then the supporting copy. The headline and the brand promise are protected positioning (owner decision, #61): both must stay visible in the hero and must not be removed or reworded without explicit owner approval.

Supporting copy:

> Fafanua designs and operates the data foundations that connect business systems, establish trust, and turn organisational data into intelligence.

## CTAs

Primary:

> **Discuss your data needs →**

Secondary:

> **Explore our capabilities**

The primary CTA should be visually dominant.

## Hero constraints

Do not crowd the hero with:

- statistics;
- multiple cards;
- client logos;
- screenshots;
- product mockups;
- speculative claims.

The hero should feel spacious and confident.

---

# 6. Header and Navigation

Upgrade the navigation to feel more deliberate.

Preferred desktop structure:

**Fafanua logo**  
Services  
Solutions  
How We Work  
About  
**Discuss a project →**

The logo should link to the top of the page.

The CTA can replace a separate Contact navigation item if the destination is clear.

## Behaviour

At page top:

- transparent over the dark hero;
- light text;
- strong contrast.

On scroll:

- transition to a solid or lightly translucent treatment;
- preserve contrast;
- avoid excessive blur.

## Mobile

Keep the mobile navigation simple, keyboard accessible, screen-reader friendly, and easy to scan.

---

# 7. Typography Upgrade

Typography should carry substantially more of the visual identity.

Suggested desktop ranges:

- Hero headline: **72–104px**
- Major section headings: **48–64px**
- Service headings: **24–28px**
- Supporting body copy: **18–20px**
- Eyebrows / labels: compact and restrained

Tune final values to the existing typeface and actual viewport behaviour.

Retain Fafanua's lighter typography direction.

Do not make the site uniformly heavy or bold.

Control line lengths carefully; large text should not span the entire viewport.

---

# 8. Positioning Section

Immediately after the hero, use a light, spacious positioning section.

Purpose:

- explain Fafanua in one concise block;
- bridge the hero and detailed service content;
- create visual breathing room.

Preferred composition:

- large statement on one side;
- supporting copy on the other;
- generous whitespace.

Do not turn this into another card.

---

# 9. Capabilities / Services

Keep the approved six service cards.

## Section numbering

Introduce restrained section numbering across major commercial sections where it improves scanning.

Suggested pattern:

- **01 — Capabilities**
- **02 — Managed Services**
- **03 — Solutions**
- **04 — How We Work**

Use small, technical, secondary typography.

Do not make section numbers visually dominant.

## Eyebrow + statement pattern

Use a consistent pattern for major sections:

**small technical eyebrow**

followed by

**large declarative heading**

Example:

> **DATA INFRASTRUCTURE & ANALYTICS**

> **From fragmented data to trusted intelligence.**

Use this pattern selectively rather than on every minor subsection.

## Introduction

Use the approved heading:

> **From fragmented data to trusted business intelligence**

Optionally use a small eyebrow:

> **Capabilities**

Increase scale and spacing around the section introduction.

## Lifecycle rail

Add a visual lifecycle above the cards:

> **Design → Connect → Model → Trust → Govern → Decide**

Number the stages:

- **01 Design**
- **02 Connect**
- **03 Model**
- **04 Trust**
- **05 Govern**
- **06 Decide**

The numbers should help the services read as a coherent Fafanua framework rather than an unrelated list of offerings.

Possible desktop treatment:

- horizontal rail;
- subtle connector line;
- optional stage numbers;
- restrained use of existing service colours.

Mobile treatment can stack or horizontally scroll if necessary.

The lifecycle should reinforce the system without overpowering the service content.

## Service-card refinement

Retain approved content and accordion behaviour.

### Capability tags

Add a small set of concise technical capability tags to each service card.

These should allow technical buyers to scan the depth of the offering without expanding the accordion.

Use approximately **3–5 tags per service**.

Suggested tags:

**Data Strategy & Platform Architecture**
- Architecture
- Cloud
- Security
- Modernisation

**Data Engineering & Integration**
- APIs
- CDC
- ERP / CRM
- Reverse ETL
- Orchestration

**Data Warehousing & Analytics Modelling**
- Dimensional
- Semantic
- Metrics
- Data Marts

**Data Quality & Reliability**
- Freshness
- Reconciliation
- Data Contracts
- Monitoring

**Data Governance & Metadata**
- Lineage
- Glossary
- Ownership
- Metadata

**Business Intelligence & Analytics**
- Dashboards
- KPIs
- Forecasting
- Segmentation

Tags should be restrained and visually subordinate to the main description.

Do not use tags as decoration or overload cards with jargon.

Improve:

- title alignment;
- equal collapsed heights within each row;
- lifecycle-label alignment;
- CTA/accordion alignment;
- spacing;
- border treatment;
- subtle hover interaction.

Appropriate hover motion:

- approximately 8–12px translation/elevation;
- subtle border or accent transition.

Avoid large shadows and bounce effects.

Retain the approved Managed Data & Analytics proposition, including:

- ongoing data engineering;
- analytics;
- reporting;

- reverse ETL;

> **Monitor · Maintain · Improve · Activate**

# 11. System / Data Flow Visual

Add one large visual that explains how Fafanua's capabilities fit together as a system.

This should become an important storytelling element, not a decorative illustration.

## Preferred conceptual flow

```text
Operational Systems
CRM · ERP · Payments · LMS · Files · APIs
          ↓
Data Engineering & Integration
          ↓
Data Warehouse & Semantic Models
          ↓
Quality + Governance
          ↓
Analytics & Reporting
          ↓
Reverse ETL / Operational Activation
```

The visual may be rendered as:

- layered architecture;
- system stack;
- isometric technical diagram;
- structured flow diagram.

The goal is to help a visitor understand:

> **how the services connect into one operating data system**

Do not create fake infrastructure screenshots.

Do not make claims about specific client environments.

## Optional “before / after” framing

If useful, show the contrast between fragmented systems and an integrated Fafanua data foundation.

### Before

```text
CRM      Payments      ERP      Excel      SaaS
 ↓           ↓          ↓         ↓         ↓
Disconnected reporting / manual reconciliation
```

### With Fafanua

```text
Connected sources
       ↓
Trusted data platform
       ↓
Governed metrics
       ↓
Analytics + operational activation
```

Keep the visual explanation simple and legible.

- **DATA SOURCES**
- **INTEGRATION LAYER**
- **TRUST LAYER**
- **DECISION LAYER**
- **ACTIVATION LAYER**

Possible supporting terms:

**TRUST LAYER**

**DECISION LAYER**
Dashboards · KPIs · Forecasting

# 18. Motion and Interaction

Use motion sparingly.

Appropriate:

- subtle hero reveal;
- low-key grid fade-in;
- small CTA arrow translation;
- service-card hover movement;
- lifecycle-line progression;
- subtle section reveal;
- restrained navigation transition on scroll.

Avoid:

- parallax-heavy effects;
- particle systems;
- autoplay video;
- large zoom effects;
- floating elements.

Motion must reinforce hierarchy rather than compete with content.

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

---

# 19. Technical Grid Pattern

Create a reusable technical-grid utility/component.

Use only where appropriate:

- hero;
- future-ready section;
- optionally a small footer/transition area.

Requirements:

- lightweight;
- preferably CSS-based;
- low contrast;
- responsive;
- no moiré effect;
- no performance penalty;
- never behind long reading sections.

---

# 20. Responsive Behaviour

Validate at minimum:

- 360px
- 768px
- 1024px
- 1440px

On smaller screens:

- scale hero typography cleanly;
- maintain subtle grid treatment;
- keep CTA targets touch-friendly;
- stack or adapt lifecycle rail appropriately;
- stack service cards naturally;
- remove artificial equal heights where inappropriate;
- convert process timeline to vertical if required;
- keep Solutions readable;
- prevent horizontal page overflow.

---

# 21. Accessibility

Maintain or improve current accessibility.

Requirements:

- semantic landmarks;
- logical heading hierarchy;
- visible focus states;
- keyboard-accessible navigation and accordions;
- accessible contrast;
- colour never used as the only indicator;
- screen-reader-friendly mobile navigation;
- descriptive CTA text;
- reduced-motion support.

Visual impact must not reduce readability.

---

# 22. Performance

Keep the redesign lightweight.

Do not add:

- large animation libraries unless clearly justified;
- background video;
- heavy component frameworks;
- large third-party scripts;
- unnecessary image assets.

Prefer:

- CSS gradients;
- CSS transitions;
- existing React structure;
- lightweight browser APIs.

Do not materially degrade Lighthouse performance.

The completed website should visually communicate a company that understands:

- infrastructure;
- systems;
- data;
- reliability;
- governance;
- analytics;
- operational execution.

The interface should communicate precision before a visitor reads every word.

The intended impression is:

> **Engineered, confident, restrained, and enterprise-ready.**

Do not chase visual novelty.

Create a stronger Fafanua website through hierarchy, typography, contrast, rhythm, whitespace, and restrained interaction while preserving the clarity and credibility of the approved positioning.
